The Law of Karma

The most basic definition of Karma is that for every action there is a reaction. Science speaks of it as the Law of Cause and Effect. However, this Law of Cause and Effect reaches beyond the measurable reach of science. Modern day science simply has not yet learned how to measure the realms existing beyond the physical arena. In truth, the Law of Karma naturally carries over beyond the limits of physical science into the mental and lower spiritual realities.

Thus, every thought, word and action has consequences; good or bad, positive or negative, etc. What you put out there via your words and actions returns in like form just like a well made, infallible boomerang.

Most individuals have an inborn, intuitive sense that Karma is real. This is natural. "Having a conscience" is a good example of the intuitive recognition of the Law of Karma and is part of the makeup of all healthy human beings.

"One of Newton's laws of motion in physics states that for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. For every flow of energy there is, and there must be, a counterflow. This is what we call cause and effect."

Paul Twitchel - the Flute of God, pg 25

Karma and Christianity

Within the Christian writings is the well known phrase, "As you sow, so you reap." This simple phrase describes Karma in a nutshell. Basically, what a person does, either good or bad, comes back to them. Further, Christianity also embraces Karma through its golden rule: "Do unto others as you would have them do unto you."

Perhaps in greater numbers than ever before people are questioning mainstream religions. However, the reader is encouraged to separate the Law of Karma and other spiritual laws from any "Religion". Often a person seeking truth through a religion can be overwhelmed by having to sift through a lot of questionable information in order to find truth. Because of this, the current tendency is to mistakenly consider that religion and spirituality are inseparable and reject both. This is unfortunate because if a person is willing to look into Karma they will find many benefits including more harmony and happiness. They can also learn how to create a better future.

Personally, I am grateful for the existence of most religions since, no matter how flawed they may or may not be, they are of immense value to society and people in general. Why? Because most provide a necessary and useful moral compass to live by. A perfect historical example is the remarkable, transformational effect that Christianity had on the Norwegian Vikings during the Viking Era. Such Christian ideas found within its doctrines such as the idea that murder and theft were wrong and the belief that; "As you sow, so you reap"(Karma in nut shell) seems to have reduced, to a remarkable degree, the violent and brutal activities that were practiced as a matter of course by many Vikings until contact with Christian doctrine occurred.

Reincarnation, A Key Component of Karma and Absolute Justice

A common argument against the idea that Karma is real are the many apparent injustices that occur in general and in our own lives. Most of us can think of examples of wrong doings going unpunished. The idea that absolute justice is woven into the fabric of reality, although true, is difficult for some to accept based on personal experiences. For example, there are occasions where the individual witnesses, hears of or experiences an injustice or a crime that is never made right. There are so many examples where a person seems to get away with something. For example a family member wrongs another family member and then passes away before justice balances the scales. It would seem to the casual observer that the person who committed the wrong doing "got away with it" through death. A historical example would be Joseph Stalin. He was responsible for the deaths of millions leading up to and during WW2 but afterward lived to old age and died without being brought to justice. Or how about the fact that some people are born healthy and others are born with defects either physical or mental? How can that be explained? Simply. It has to do with the eternal nature of the individual, Soul. We all existed prior to this life and we all continue to exist after this life. Reincarnation is the key to absolute justice. Karma is responsible for the conditions prior to birth and after death. Over and over and over again. As we progress through time we eventually balance the scales of Karma and become free from reincarnating into the physical worlds. This is spiritual freedom. Working out our Karma with others is one important aspect of how this freedom is achieved. Further, this is how perfect justice works itself out over time, over hundreds and thousands of lifetimes.

Nobody gets away with anything, ever.

Nobody gets away with anything, ever. If a person wrongs another, the wrong-doer will generally be subjected to similar circumstances through reincarnation or other spiritual means. There exists perfect justice in the universe and reincarnation is the key.
